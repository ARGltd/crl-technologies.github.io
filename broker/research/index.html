The conclusion to present

In the simulated comparison, CRL beats traditional 5× leverage on severe losses and client capital in all 18 variants at baseline pricing. A-book earns more at the model coverage price; in B-book, the broker chooses the price and the trade-off.

Overview conditions and notes

24-month comparison, starting capital of $50,000: averages across 18 correlated historical variants. Charts and table use baseline pricing, with no additional voluntary exits. Q + 5% and Q + 7.5% are separate pricing scenarios. These are simulated results, not commercial promises.

The coverage price is still a model price. On average, the A-book advantage holds up to about 0.60% above it (0.35–0.89% across variants). A real quote is the next step.

Conditions: provider priced at the theoretical premium Q, disclosed costs, A-book execution stress of 5 seconds + 0.5 pip. The B-book retains all risk; no cost of capital is included. No revenue from retention has been added.

The cost of leverage

Leverage is financed: interest is charged on the full position for every night it is held. Before the trigger a CRL position is 1×, so it carries one fifth of the financing of a traditional 5× position; after the trigger it carries the same. The study charges 3% a year on notional to both products. The capital comparison depends on this cost.

Spread and broker revenue

In the study, in A-book, the broker adds no spread markup and retains no financing, for either product: both pay the same market spread. The rules are the same, but the effect is not neutral. A markup is earned on volume, and a traditional 5× client trades on average about 4.4 times the volume of a CRL client: the more a broker earns per million traded, the more traditional leverage is worth to it.

Under the presented execution stress, the CRL A-book margin advantage holds up to an additional markup of about 0.08 pip per side on average (0.04–0.11 across variants), or up to about 0.32% a year of financing retained (0.20–0.41%). Under baseline execution: about 0.24 pip (0.17–0.32) and about 0.98% a year (0.82–1.24%). A broker that earns mainly from spread can set the CRL service fee or premium accordingly; this moves value from client to broker, as in the B-book pricing scenarios. For the client the effect runs the other way: a markup costs the 5× client more.

Fewer severe losses do not mean fewer clients below starting capital: at month 24, 837.3 CRL clients versus 798.5 traditional per 1,000. Higher commercial retention has not yet been demonstrated.

The replacement analysis, which includes replacement-client margins and acquisition costs, found no tested configuration combining higher average client capital, a longer relationship and positive B-book margin above traditional. Exit scenarios do not yet demonstrate actual commercial lifetime.

How to read “Average client wealth”. The chart above uses the baseline premium. In tests with a higher premium, the CRL curve can fall below traditional: this is the cost of that pricing variant, not the baseline-pricing result. The two configurations are kept separate in the detailed analysis.

The strength of the work lies in comparing the same cohorts and reconciling cash flows. The cohort is a controlled 5× leverage scenario, not an empirically calibrated retail population. Rules, costs, sources and limitations are integral to the assessment.

What it offers the broker

In the simulated comparison, the client-capital benefit is accompanied by higher broker margin than traditional leverage in all 18 variants under the stated conditions. CRL A-book margin comes from the 2.75 bp service fee and commissions, including the exit commission calculated on 5× notional, net of included costs. This provides a concrete basis for comparing the economic budget with broker and provider terms.

When it makes economic sense

The client already pays Q; the additional budget is the headroom for a higher coverage cost before reaching parity with traditional leverage. Under the presented stress (5 seconds + 0.5 pip), the average advantage reaches zero at approximately 0.60% above Q: thresholds across variants are 0.35–0.89% of Q, not percentage points of capital. Under baseline execution, the average threshold is approximately 1.9% above Q (1.45–2.27% across variants). These thresholds hold client pricing, trades and other costs fixed; they indicate parity with traditional leverage, not zero CRL margin. Different client pricing requires a separate assessment.

What was verified

PURE B-BOOK · PRICING SENSITIVITY

Higher premium.
Fixed trigger. 5× leverage.

The additional premium increases broker margin, but reduces the client's net result. CRL B-book is already positive on average at the disclosed pricing; this test measures whether it can approach traditional while preserving the client advantage.

With a 5% loading on Q, average B-book margin is $10,813: $4,272 more than CRL without a loading. Average client wealth is $37,882, a difference of -$32 versus traditional. The broker remains $620 below traditional. These are average results: the client outperforms traditional in 12/18 variants, the broker in 6/18, and both within the same variant in 0/18.

Q + 5% means a $100 premium becomes $105. It does not mean 5% of capital or a 5% stop. The loading is paid at every opening, even when the trigger is not reached, and is retained by the broker.

The premium transfers value from client to counterparty. In a pure B-book, the identity is: client wealth + broker margin + included costs = initial capital. The benefit in loss severity must be read separately from average capital and the number of clients making a loss.

More margin and fewer severe losses: a measurable trade-off

At Q + 7.5%, average B-book margin is $12,783 ($1,349 above traditional). Clients crossing a 50% loss threshold number 33.3 per 1,000, versus 371.3 under traditional. The higher premium reduces average client capital: average wealth of $35,940 versus $37,914; clients below initial capital of 978.2 versus 798.5. Higher broker margin and fewer −50% threshold crossings coexist in 17/18 variants. This combines higher margin with less severe losses, not higher margin with higher average client capital.

Protocol, excluded pricing levels and test limitations

Original trigger z = 0.35, 5× leverage, market histories, profiles, random draws, directions, commissions and traditional rules unchanged. Capital, daily results and re-entry decisions recalculated for each pricing level. Contract exits are reused after verification of the duration rule.

17 admissible pricing levels × 18 variants = 306 cohort paths, 306,000 client paths and 7,344,000 client-months. These are correlated replays of three historical windows, not observed clients or 306 independent experiments. The grid and pricing-range extension were fixed before the new economic results.

All potential triggered contracts were checked, including those not selected by clients. Admitted pricing levels retain a positive net result in the sample. Near the limit, the residual is very small: historical positivity is not a future guarantee. Demand, price elasticity, commercial churn, risk capital, taxes and undisclosed costs are not estimated. No pricing level is a commercial recommendation.

The zero-loading replay was reconciled to the original independent ledgers; cash identities, absence of overlapping positions and positive trigger outcomes were checked internally. The new test has not yet undergone independent review.

B-BOOK ANALYSIS · LIFETIME AND PROFITABILITY

More time for the client.
Under what conditions does it pay?

Actual relationship duration is distinct from capital preserved. This analysis extends only the B-book; A-book and hybrid models retain the conditions disclosed in their respective sections.

At the technical threshold of $500, nobody exits within 24 months: no lifetime extension emerges relative to traditional. In the explicit scenario of exit after a realised loss of 50%, with premium Q +7.5%, restricted mean duration over 24 months is 23.93 months versus 21.55; 970.6 clients per 1,000 remain versus 647.9. B-book margin is $12,762 versus $10,846 per initial client. Longer duration and higher margin coexist in 17/18 variants. This result is conditional on the exit rule, not an estimate of actual churn. Average client wealth, including withdrawals, is $35,963 versus $38,536: longer duration does not imply higher final capital.

Exits here are loss-tolerance scenarios: the same threshold applies to both products. After the trade that takes the balance below the threshold, the client withdraws the remaining capital and opens no further positions. No new deposits or replacement clients are introduced.

Duration is restricted to the 24 observed months; clients who do not exit contribute 24 months. This is neither full average lifetime nor a commercial-retention forecast. Thresholds use the post-close balance, not the minimum during a position.

How much must avoided replacement be worth?

Initial value zero: no benefit added. V is the average value over the same horizon that is not already included in the ledger. It must subtract the net margin forgone on replacement clients from the replacement cost avoided. CAC alone is not V. This is an economic threshold, not a replacement simulation or an LTV estimate. V does not change charts, balances or exits.

Method, evidence and checks

17 admissible pricing levels × 18 pairs × 6 exit rules. Previously verified ledgers are truncated at the first exit event; before that event, every trade and decision remains identical. No new calibration of leverage, stops, trigger, market or behaviour.

Sources document relationships between results, activity and cessation, but do not calibrate our exit thresholds or their prevalence among clients. Source CB01 and the documentary audit distinguish relative hazard, cessation, withdrawals and ruin. CB03 concerns another population and is not used to impose a Forex curve. No empirical churn rate has been invented.

Verified: no-exit replay, return of remaining capital, counts, censored durations and the monthly identity: client wealth + B-book margin + included costs = initial capital. The independent review covers the baseline; this extension has internal checks. The package retains all 18 variants, all pricing levels and every month.

Outcome: The economic assessment conditional on exit rules is complete. An extension of actual commercial lifetime has not been demonstrated; exit frequencies and net replacement value for the broker's population are needed to turn the sensitivity into a forecast.

A CHOICE OF METHODOLOGICAL DISCIPLINE

A disciplined traditional benchmark.
A demanding comparison for CRL.

To avoid constructing a CRL advantage by artificially penalising its comparator, the traditional benchmark imposes discipline constraints. The dossiers also describe more aggressive behaviours; these are not combined here into a scenario designed to produce ruin.

One position at a time

No overlapping positions that multiply total account exposure. The effect of simultaneous portfolios is not attributed to the result.

5× leverage, without escalation

No increase in leverage after a loss and no doubling down to recover losses. Notional is five times remaining capital: it decreases when the account loses value.

Duration and execution limits

Stops and time-based closes are applied by the rules, without discretionary cancellation. Extensions of losing positions are capped; a loss is not left open indefinitely.

What this approach implies. These constraints limit the accumulation of exposure and some channels of extreme loss. For the same adverse move, greater exposure would amplify the loss. However, the number of additional accounts that would be depleted has not been measured: trades, costs and favourable outcomes would also change. The disciplined traditional benchmark does not represent the entire population described in the dossiers and is not a guaranteed statistical bound.

The comparison includes fixed activity and more frequent re-entry after a positive day. This behavioural transfer is disclosed; it is not a full calibration of retail clients. Dossiers CB01, CB02, CB05 and CB06 .

Exposure Traditional: notional of 5× remaining capital. CRL: 1× before the trigger, payoff amplified to 5× at the trigger. One position per client at a time. Lower downside exposure is a property of the product comparison; its benefit alone does not establish the added value of the premium and amplification.

Durations Declared profiles: 4 / 8 / 16 / 24 / 48 hours. Initial durations applied by index: 1 / 4 / 8 / 16 / 24 hours. Losing positions extended up to 120 hours; close on Friday at 20:00 UTC or earlier at the specified limits. The mapping between profile and initial duration is explicit but not empirically calibrated. Time-based closes limit prolonged losses.
Behaviour Fixed activity or increased re-entry probability after a win. The coefficient originally relating to volume is applied to entry probability. A transferred mechanism, not a full calibration. No recovery of losses through higher leverage; after a loss, notional decreases with capital.
Churn Voluntary churn disabled; technical remaining-capital threshold of $500 in the engine. No new clients, deposits or withdrawals in the model. The 1,000 clients still trading do not constitute an empirical survival curve. Preserved capital is not automatically translated into retention.

A-book broker with included costs only. Funding favours the client comparison when exposure decreases. The broker's funding/spread share, the provider's executable price and cost of capital can reverse the advantage.
CRL execution The client's net result is locked in at the trigger. Subsequent execution deviations fall on the broker. Four stresses applied to the same client paths. Execution risk is transferred to the broker; the four stresses are not four independent client samples.
Why the sources do not translate directly into these curves

The dossiers document erosion, repeated activity and churn in populations with different instruments, leverage and definitions. They do not provide joint calibration for our 5× EUR/USD profiles. The current model imposes duration, size, sequencing and exit policy: these constraints contribute to the outcome. The gap versus the sources cannot be corrected by choosing a target churn rate or loss.

For an adverse move d and an invested fraction f of capital, the gross pre-trigger loss is approximately f·d for CRL and 5·f·d for STD, plus their respective costs and the CRL premium. This is a mechanical relationship: it does not identify the distribution of d, chosen stops, trade frequency or survival.

Hayley and Marsh · What do retail FX traders learn?

In retail FX, the relative probability of stopping is associated with recent results; after a positive day, the hazard is 16% lower than after a negative day.

The US leverage restriction is associated with an estimated 23% reduction in volume and an approximately 40% reduction in losses among highly leveraged traders.

In the Taiwan equity day-trader sample, survival is 44% at one year and 24% at two years, under specific entry and cessation criteria.
